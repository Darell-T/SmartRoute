# NYC Transit Facts

Curated reference facts for grounding rider questions about fares, transfers,
service patterns, and accessibility policy. This file is static and hand
curated -- it is not a live data source. For anything that changes day to
day (delays, elevator outages, event schedules, live arrivals), use the
live tools instead of this file.

## Fares & OMNY

- Effective January 4, 2026, the base subway and local bus fare is $3.00 per
  ride. Source: MTA 2026 Fare Change Materials
  (https://www.mta.info/document/186881).
- Use OMNY (a contactless card, phone, smartwatch, or MTA-issued OMNY card)
  for subway and local-bus fare payment. MetroCard sales ended with the 2026
  fare change; do not recommend buying an unlimited-ride MetroCard.
- OMNY has a rolling seven-day fare cap of $35.00 for eligible subway and
  local-bus taps. The cap is not a Monday-Sunday or 12-ride rule.
- Reduced-fare programs exist for seniors, riders with qualifying
  disabilities, and eligible low-income riders (Fair Fares NYC).
  Enrollment and eligibility are handled by MTA/NYC programs directly, not
  by this app -- point riders to mta.info for enrollment.
- Express buses, AirTrain JFK, and the Staten Island Railway all have their
  own fares, separate from the $3.00 local subway/bus fare -- never assume
  the base fare applies to those.

## Transfers

- A free transfer between subway and local bus, or between two local
  buses, is available within 2 hours of the first tap when paying with
  OMNY or MetroCard.
- Transfers between subway lines at stations that are physically connected
  in-system (same fare-control area) are free and require no additional
  tap.
- Leaving a station's fare-control area and re-entering at an unconnected
  station is a new fare unless it falls within an applicable transfer
  window -- do not assume every station-to-station move is free.
- Express buses have their own transfer rules and a fare differential from
  local buses -- do not assume the local bus/subway transfer rule applies
  to express bus trips.

## Service Hours

- The subway runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week -- there is no
  system-wide overnight shutdown.
- Overnight (roughly midnight to 5am) service on many lines is less
  frequent, and some lines that run express during the day switch to a
  local stopping pattern overnight.
- A handful of lines do not run overnight or on weekends at all -- for
  example the W does not run overnight, and the B does not run nights or
  weekends. Always tell a rider planning a late-night or weekend trip to
  double check the specific line's current schedule rather than assuming
  every line runs on the same pattern.
- Planned service changes (track work, station closures) are common on
  nights and weekends specifically -- see the Service Alerts section below.

## Accessibility Basics

- Not every subway station has an elevator; roughly 30% of stations are
  currently considered ADA accessible (elevator or ramp access from
  street to platform) -- treat this as an approximate figure, not an exact
  count, since accessible-station upgrades are ongoing.
- Where a station does have elevators, they can be out of service --
  always check current elevator/escalator status for a station before
  telling a rider with a cart, stroller, or wheelchair to use it,
  especially for a route with a transfer.
- Escalator outages are inconvenient but not usually blocking; elevator
  outages can make a station effectively inaccessible for a wheelchair or
  a rider who cannot use stairs -- treat elevator outages as the higher
  priority to surface.
- Accessible stations are marked with the wheelchair-access symbol on MTA
  maps and signage; this file does not maintain a station-by-station list,
  so defer to the live elevator/escalator status tool for a specific
  station rather than guessing whether it is accessible.

## Airport Connections

- AirTrain JFK connects JFK Airport to the subway/LIRR at Jamaica Station
  and to the A train at Howard Beach; AirTrain JFK has its own fare,
  separate from and in addition to the subway/LIRR fare for the rest of
  the trip.
- LaGuardia Airport (LGA) has no rail link. The Q70 LaGuardia Link SBS bus
  connects LGA to the 7 train (Jackson Heights-Roosevelt Ave) and the
  E/M/R at Roosevelt Ave -- it is a normal-fare Select Bus Service route,
  not a free shuttle.
- Newark Airport (EWR) is served by AirTrain Newark to NJ Transit/Amtrak
  at Newark Airport Station -- this is an NJ Transit/Amtrak fare zone, not
  an MTA subway fare.

## Boroughs, Express & Local

- The subway serves Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx; Staten
  Island is served by the separate Staten Island Railway plus the Staten
  Island Ferry connection to Manhattan.
- "Express" trains skip local stops along a shared trunk line to run
  faster over a distance; "local" trains stop at every station on a line.
  The same numbered/lettered line can run express in one segment and
  local in another, and express service is often reduced or suspended
  overnight -- always check current routing rather than assuming a line's
  daytime express pattern holds at all hours.

## Staten Island Ferry

- The Staten Island Ferry between St. George (Staten Island) and Whitehall
  Street (Manhattan) is free for all riders, with no fare or OMNY/MetroCard
  tap required.
- The ferry is not part of the subway/bus OMNY fare system, so it has no
  bearing on a rider's weekly OMNY cap either way.

## Regional Rail (LIRR / Metro-North / NJ Transit)

- The Long Island Rail Road (LIRR), Metro-North Railroad, and NJ Transit
  are separate commuter rail systems with their own fares and schedules --
  none of them are covered by subway/bus OMNY or MetroCard fare.
- All three connect to the subway at shared hubs (notably Penn Station and
  Grand Central for LIRR/Metro-North, and Penn Station for NJ Transit),
  but a rider needs a separate ticket for the commuter-rail leg.
- MetLife Stadium (East Rutherford, NJ) is reached via NJ Transit from
  Penn Station or Port Authority, not via the subway -- see the agent's
  MetLife/New Jersey handling for routing guidance on this specific case.

## Service Alerts

- The MTA publishes planned service changes (track work, station closures,
  rerouted trains) that are especially common overnight and on weekends;
  always treat a stated schedule as subject to change and prefer a live
  alerts/status check over an assumption when one is available.
- Unplanned disruptions (signal problems, medical emergencies, police
  activity) can also affect service at any time -- these show up in live
  alert/incident tools, not in this static reference file.
